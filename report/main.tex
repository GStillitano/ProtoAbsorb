\begin{abstract}
Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) keeps a deployed model accurate under
distribution shift, but in the open world it must also keep separating
in-distribution inputs from semantically novel out-of-distribution (OOD)
ones. We study how a standard entropy-minimisation method
(\method{Tent}) trades off these two goals over time: as adaptation
proceeds it stays accurate, but its ability to detect OOD inputs steadily
degrades. We trace this to the geometry of the learned embeddings,
tracking how their properties drift as the model adapts and showing that
this drift is what erases the signal OOD detectors rely on. Guided by
this analysis, we introduce \method{NOVA-TTA}
(Normal-Oriented Vector Alignment), a TTA method that adapts
likely-in-distribution and likely-OOD inputs differently so that the
model keeps improving under shift without losing the geometric signal
that separates novel inputs. On CIFAR-10-C, \method{NOVA-TTA} preserves
OOD detection where \method{Tent} degrades, at no meaningful accuracy
cost.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) updates a deployed model online, without
labels, as the test stream drifts from the training distribution. The
canonical method, \method{Tent}~\cite{tent}, minimises predictive entropy
batch by batch, updating only the affine parameters of the
batch-normalisation (BN) layers.

Realistic streams are \emph{open-set}: alongside corrupted known-class
inputs (\emph{covariate-shifted in-distribution}, \csid) they contain
semantically novel ones (\emph{covariate-shifted out-of-distribution},
\csood) that the model should reject. The prevailing remedy is
\emph{split-and-oppose}: estimate per-sample membership from a score,
minimise entropy on the presumed \csid{} subset, oppose it on the
presumed \csood{} subset.

This framing hides a geometric failure present even with a perfect split.
With the head frozen, entropy minimisation can only stretch the feature
norm or rotate its angle to the class weights. The norm gap is already
weak under covariate shift, so what gives way is the angle: \csood{}
inputs re-align onto their nearest weight and drift into the source
classes until energy-based detection can no longer separate them from
\csid. Detectability degrades regardless of split quality.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{Protocol.} A two-phase protocol that tracks OOD and
  geometric metrics over the adaptation stream, isolating persistent
  affine drift from transient BN statistics.
  \item \textbf{Analysis.} A geometric account of \emph{why} closed-set
  TTA degrades open-set performance: not the weak norm gap but an
  \emph{angular} collapse---\csood{} re-aligns and is absorbed into the
  source classes.
  \item \textbf{Method.} \method{NOVA-TTA}, a soft open-set TTA framework
  derived from the analysis, which empirically reverses the AUROC
  collapse.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Test-Time Adaptation.} \method{Tent}~\cite{tent} established
entropy minimisation over BN affine parameters as a lightweight baseline;
\emph{BN Adapt}~\cite{bnadapt} is the gradient-free special case.
\method{UniEnt}~\cite{unient} extends TTA to the open set with a hard
ID/OOD split and opposing per-subset objectives. We share the goal but
locate the failure in the geometry of the affine update, not the split.

\paragraph{OOD detection.} Energy-based scoring~\cite{energy} reads a
log-partition energy off the logits (Eq.~\eqref{eq:energy}); its power
rests on an \csid/\csood{} gap in logit magnitude, dominated by the
feature norm. We use the energy-score AUROC as the detectability metric.

\paragraph{Feature geometry.} The feature norm is a known ID/OOD
discriminator~\cite{featurenorm}: OOD inputs tend to smaller-norm
embeddings. Relative representations encode a sample by its
cosine similarities to fixed anchors and are invariant to
rescaling---precisely what a norm-dominant adaptation leaves untouched.
This motivates our norm-invariant alignment score.

\paragraph{Benchmark and backbone.} We evaluate on CIFAR-10-C, the
corruption benchmark standardised by \method{RobustBench}~\cite{robustbench},
and load its WideResNet backbone through the \method{RobustBench} model
zoo. The backbone is trained with \method{AugMix}~\cite{augmix}, a data
augmentation scheme that mixes randomly composed transforms to improve
robustness and calibration under corruption---a representative,
corruption-robust source model for the open-set TTA setting.

\section{TTA in the Closed and Open Set}\label{sec:tta}

\paragraph{Test-time adaptation.} Under covariate shift (noise, blur,
weather) a source-trained model loses accuracy. TTA recovers it
\emph{online}---no labels, no source data---updating only a small subset
$\theta\subset\Theta$ of parameters from an unlabelled stream of
size-$N$ batches $\mathcal{B}_1\!\to\!\mathcal{B}_2\!\to\!\cdots$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:stream}).

\paragraph{Entropy minimisation (\method{Tent}).} A $K$-class
classifier maps an input $x$ to logits $\psi(x)\in\R^K$ and a predictive
distribution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:softmax}
p(x)=\softmax\big(\psi(x)\big),
\qquad
p_{k}(x)=\frac{e^{\psi_k(x)}}{\sum_{j=1}^{K}e^{\psi_j(x)}} .
\end{equation}
\method{Tent}~\cite{tent} adapts \emph{only} the affine scale and shift
of the batch-normalisation layers,
$\theta=(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})\subset\Theta$, acting channel-wise as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bn}
\mathrm{BN}(z)=\bm{\gamma}\odot
\frac{z-\bm{\mu}_{\mathcal{B}}}{\bm{\sigma}_{\mathcal{B}}}+\bm{\beta},
\end{equation}
with statistics $(\bm{\mu}_{\mathcal{B}},\bm{\sigma}_{\mathcal{B}})$
recomputed per batch and never persisted; only $(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})$
accumulate, the head and convolutional weights stay frozen. Each batch
takes one gradient step on the mean predictive entropy,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tent}
\theta_t=\theta_{t-1}-\eta\,\nabla_{\theta}\,
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{B}_t}H\big(p(x)\big),
\qquad
H(p)=-\sum_{k=1}^{K}p_k\log p_k,
\end{equation}
sharpening $p$ on the shifted inputs and recovering most of the accuracy
lost to corruption.

\paragraph{Closed vs.\ open set.} \method{Tent} is designed for the
\emph{closed set}: every batch holds \csid{} inputs only, no \csood. In
practice the stream is \emph{open}---each input is a two-component
mixture, \csid{} with probability $1-\alpha$ and \csood{} (unknown
classes) with probability $\alpha$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixture}
x_i\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}(1-\alpha)\,P_{\mathrm{ID}}
+\alpha\,P_{\mathrm{OOD}},
\qquad \alpha\in[0,1],
\end{equation}
so $\alpha=0$ recovers the closed set. For $\alpha>0$ the goal is now
double: raise accuracy on \csid{} while keeping \csood{} detection high
(\S\ref{sec:metrics}). The two conflict: every batch \method{Tent} adapts
on is itself a mixture, and the entropy step \eqref{eq:tent} sharpens
\csood{} as well. Since standard OOD scores---confidence, entropy,
energy---read exactly the peakedness entropy minimisation drives up,
detection erodes (Fig.~\ref{fig:openclosed}). \S\ref{sec:failure} makes
this precise.

\section{Understanding the Failure of \method{Tent}}\label{sec:failure}

\subsection{Last-layer geometry}\label{sec:background}

Let $\g:\mathcal{X}\to\R^d$ be the penultimate embedding and
$\mathbf{W}=[\mathbf{w}_1,\dots,\mathbf{w}_K]^\top\in\R^{K\times d}$ the
frozen linear head with bias $\mathbf{b}$, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logits-def}
\psi(x)=\mathbf{W}\g(x)+\mathbf{b},
\qquad
\psi_k(x)=\mathbf{w}_k^\top\g(x)+b_k .
\end{equation}
Each $\mathbf{w}_k$ is the normal of the class-$k$ decision hyperplane;
the prediction is $c^*(x)=\argmax_k\psi_k(x)$. The winning logit in polar
form gives the decomposition that organises the analysis,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logit}
\psi_{c^*}(x)=
\underbrace{\norm{\mathbf{w}_{c^*}}\,\norm{\g(x)}\,\cos\theta_{c^*}}
_{\text{norm}\,\times\,\text{alignment}}+\,b_{c^*},
\qquad
\cos\theta_{c^*}=\frac{\mathbf{w}_{c^*}^\top\g(x)}
{\norm{\mathbf{w}_{c^*}}\,\norm{\g(x)}} .
\end{equation}
With $\mathbf{W},\mathbf{b}$ frozen, the only levers entropy minimisation
can pull on $\psi_{c^*}$ are the feature norm $\norm{\g(x)}$ and the
alignment $\cos\theta_{c^*}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry}).

\subsection{OOD detection and entropy}\label{sec:ood}

Post-hoc OOD scores read the same peakedness as entropy. Confidence,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conf}
C(x)=\max_k p_{k}(x),
\end{equation}
and energy~\cite{energy}, the log-partition of the logits,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
\Energy(x)=-\log\sum_{k=1}^{K}e^{\psi_k(x)},
\end{equation}
are both monotone in the predictive entropy $H(p(x))$. A detector
$s(x)=-\Energy(x)$ flags low-entropy inputs as \csid; it works only
because \csid{} sits at lower entropy than \csood,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ent-gap}
\E_{P_{\mathrm{OOD}}}H\big(p(x)\big)>\E_{P_{\mathrm{ID}}}H\big(p(x)\big),
\end{equation}
\method{Tent} minimises $H$ over the whole mixed batch \eqref{eq:tent},
\csood{} included, driving $\E_{P_{\mathrm{OOD}}}H$ toward
$\E_{P_{\mathrm{ID}}}H$: the gap closes and detectability collapses.

\subsection{How \method{Tent} reshapes embeddings}\label{sec:predict}

The entropy step \eqref{eq:tent} updates only the BN affine parameters
$(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})$; the head $\mathbf{W},\mathbf{b}$ and the
convolutional weights stay frozen. So the sole thing \method{Tent} can
change is the embedding $\g(x)$---the logit \eqref{eq:logit} is a fixed
function of it. Minimising $H$ sharpens the softmax, i.e.\ reshapes
$\g(x)$ to \emph{maximise} the winning logit $\psi_{c^*}(x)$, and by the
decomposition \eqref{eq:logit} an embedding moves it only two ways:
\emph{norm inflation} ($\norm{\g(x)}\!\uparrow$) or \emph{re-alignment}
($\theta_{c^*}\!\downarrow$). Both push $\g(x)$ deeper into the positive
half-space---raising the signed distance $d$ to the hyperplane and, since
$\psi_{c^*}=\norm{\mathbf{w}_{c^*}}\,d$, the logit itself
(Fig.~\ref{fig:levers}). Both act on \csood{} as much as on \csid,
since $(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})$ are shared across the whole batch.

geometry---\csood{} at smaller norm and weaker alignment---do the two
populations move along \emph{different} levers, or along the same one?

\section{Experiment on the Failure of \method{Tent}}\label{sec:experiment}

\subsection{Time-tracking protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

We track how \method{Tent} reshapes a fixed dataset as adaptation
proceeds. Since adaptation is sequential, we separate \emph{adapting} the
model from \emph{measuring} it, so a metric reflects the parameter
trajectory, not the batch it was read on (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol}).

\paragraph{Adapt.} Run \method{Tent} on the open-set stream
$\mathcal{B}_1\!\to\!\cdots\!\to\!\mathcal{B}_T$, generating the sequence
of iterates $(\theta_t)_{t=0}^{T}$ produced by the update \eqref{eq:tent}.

\paragraph{Evaluate.} Hold each iterate $\theta_t$ fixed and evaluate it on
one fixed set $\D=\D_{\csid}\cup\D_{\csood}$, never adapted on, so the
trajectory $m(0),\dots,m(T)$ isolates the per-step drift
$\theta_{t-1}\!\to\!\theta_t$. We
split $\D$ into $J$ small mixed batches $\D^{(j)}$ and average the
metric, reproducing the regime \method{Tent} adapts under: open-set
batches, small and mixed, with noisy statistics. Batch normalisation
rescales each batch by its own statistics, so a large or
single-population batch would wash out exactly that signal.

\subsection{Metrics}\label{sec:metrics}

\S\ref{sec:predict} left two questions: as the winning logit rises, how
does it split between \emph{norm} and \emph{alignment}, and do \csid{} and
\csood{} move along the same lever? The metrics read exactly these two
axes, plus the detection outcome they drive. Each is a population mean
$\E_{\D_\bullet}$ over $\bullet\in\{\csid,\csood\}$ of a fixed iterate
$\theta_t$; $c^*_0(x)=\argmax_k\psi_0(x)$ is the source-predicted class,
fixed at $t{=}0$.

\paragraph{Accuracy--detection trade-off.} The two goals open-set TTA must
hold together. Accuracy on \csid,
$\mathrm{Acc}(t)=\E_{\D_{\csid}}\Ind[c^*_t(x)=y]$, and the energy-score
detectability $\AUROC(t)$ under $s=-\Energy$---the probability an \csid{}
input scores more ID-like than an \csood{} one. Together they say whether
adaptation keeps accuracy while preserving the OOD signal.

\paragraph{Norms and gap.} Per-population mean $L_2$ and $L_1$ feature
norms, $\E_{\D_\bullet}\norm{\gt(x)}_2$ and $\E_{\D_\bullet}\norm{\gt(x)}_1$,
and the \csid$-$\csood{} \emph{norm gap}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
\Delta(t)=\E_{\D_{\csid}}\norm{\gt(x)}-\E_{\D_{\csood}}\norm{\gt(x)}
\end{equation}
($\Delta_2,\Delta_1$ per norm). Energy detection works only while
$\Delta>0$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:metrics}, right).

\paragraph{Alignment and semantics.} The angular lever. Cosine to the
source-predicted class $\E_{\D_\bullet}[\cos\theta_{c^*_0(x)}]$; the
norm-invariant \emph{max cosine}
$\E_{\D_\bullet}[\max_k\cos\angle(\gt(x),\mathbf{w}_k)]$, the alignment to
the nearest class weight (Eq.~\eqref{eq:score}; Fig.~\ref{fig:metrics},
left); and the distance to the nearest \emph{frozen} source centroid,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centroid}
\E_{\D_\bullet}\min_{c}\norm{\gt(x)-\bm{\mu}_c^{(0)}},
\qquad
\bm{\mu}_c^{(0)}=\E\big[\gz(x)\mid y=c\big]\ \text{(clean ID)},
\end{equation}
which tells re-alignment apart from genuine semantic movement. We also
track \csood{} confidence $\E_{\D_{\csood}}C(x)$.

the diagnostics show which one carries the decay---and it is not the
obvious one.

\section{Our Proposal: \method{NOVA-TTA}}\label{sec:nova-tta}

\subsection{A general framework}\label{sec:framework}

Most open-set TTA methods instantiate one pipeline
(Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}): each sample gets a \emph{score}, the batch of
scores becomes a per-sample OOD \emph{posterior}, the posterior a
\emph{labelling}, the labelling the adaptation \emph{loss},
$s(x)\to\pi(\mathrm{OOD}\mid x)\to\ell_i\to\mathcal{L}(\ell)$. A method is
fixed by four choices: score, posterior, labelling, and loss.

% --- framework pipeline figure (non-floating: stays right here) ------
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[>=stealth,
   stage/.style={draw, rounded corners=2pt, fill=blue!6, font=\small,
                 minimum height=0.95cm, minimum width=2.1cm, align=center}]
  \node[stage] (s)    at (0,0)    {score\\[1pt]$s(x)$};
  \node[stage] (pi)   at (3.5,0)  {posterior\\[1pt]$\pi(\mathrm{OOD}\mid x)$};
  \node[stage] (lab)  at (7.0,0)  {labelling\\[1pt]$\ell_i$};
  \node[stage] (loss) at (10.1,0) {loss\\[1pt]$\mathcal{L}(\ell)$};
  \draw[->] (s)   -- (pi)  node[midway, above, font=\scriptsize, gray]{GMM};
  \draw[->] (pi)  -- (lab) node[midway, above, font=\scriptsize, gray]{hard/soft};
  \draw[->] (lab) -- (loss);
  \node[gray, font=\scriptsize, anchor=north] at (0,-0.72) {frozen $\theta_0$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\captionof{figure}{\textbf{The open-set TTA pipeline.} Score, OOD posterior,
labelling, loss; methods differ only in these four choices.}
\label{fig:framework}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Score.} Each sample $x_i\in\mathcal{B}_t$ gets a scalar
$s_i=s(x_i)$ separating \csid{} from \csood. This stage is the freest: any
\csood-ranking statistic is admissible---confidence, entropy, energy,
feature norm, prototype alignment---so it holds the widest design space. The
score is \emph{usually read off the original, un-adapted weights} $\theta_0$,
not the current iterate $\theta_t$: $\theta_0$ retains the OOD-detection
power that adaptation erodes (\S\ref{sec:failure}), so a frozen scorer stays
informative and comparable along the stream rather than being corrupted by
the adaptation it supervises.

\paragraph{Posterior.} A two-component Gaussian mixture (one \csid, one
\csood) is fitted by EM to the batch scores $\{s_i\}_{i\in\mathcal{B}_t}$;
Bayes' rule then gives each sample a posterior
$\pi(\mathrm{OOD}\mid x_i)\in[0,1]$.

\paragraph{Labelling.} The posterior is converted into the membership
$\ell_i$ the loss acts on, either \emph{hard} or \emph{soft},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:label}
\ell_i=
\begin{cases}
\Ind\!\big[\pi(\mathrm{OOD}\mid x_i)>\tau\big] & \text{(hard split)},\\[3pt]
\pi(\mathrm{OOD}\mid x_i)\in[0,1] & \text{(soft weight)}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The choice sets how aggressively the loss commits per sample: a hard split
applies the \csid/\csood{} loss terms at full strength across $\tau$ but
propagates every misassignment, while a soft weight scales each contribution
by its posterior, hedging under uncertainty. Either way $\ell_i$ gates a loss
that adapts on likely-\csid{} and opposes on likely-\csood.

\paragraph{\method{UniEnt} and \method{UniEnt+}.} Both score by
\emph{alignment to the source class centroids} on the frozen network and fit
the ID/OOD mixture as above; they differ only in labelling---\method{UniEnt}
hard ($\ell_i\in\{0,1\}$), \method{UniEnt+} soft ($\ell_i\in[0,1]$).

The stages of Fig.~\ref{fig:framework} with each choice made concrete: a

$\ell_i=\pi(\mathrm{OOD}\mid x_i)$; and a split loss---entropy on

\label{fig:nova-tta}

\paragraph{Score: frozen, norm-invariant alignment.} A \emph{frozen} scorer
(original weights $\theta_0$, no gradient) produces the max cosine to the
class weights,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score}
s_i=\max_k \frac{\g(x_i)\cdot\mathbf{w}_k}
{\norm{\g(x_i)}\,\norm{\mathbf{w}_k}} .
\end{equation}
It is the one statistic the analysis singled out as both discriminative at
$t{=}0$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:maxcos}) and immune to adaptation: norm-invariant, so
it ignores norm inflation, and frozen, so \csood{} cannot re-align under it.
Scores stay stationary across the stream---unlike the energy score, corrupted
by the very adaptation it would supervise.

\paragraph{Posterior: soft labelling.} Pool the scores seen up to step $t$,
$\mathcal{S}_t=\bigcup_{\tau\le t}\{s_i\}_{i\in\mathcal{B}_\tau}$, and fit by
EM a two-component Gaussian mixture
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gmm}
p(s)=\sum_{c\in\{\mathrm{id},\mathrm{ood}\}}
\pi_c\,\mathcal{N}\!\big(s\mid\mu_c,\sigma_c^2\big),
\qquad
\mu_{\mathrm{id}}>\mu_{\mathrm{ood}} ,
\end{equation}
the higher-mean component being ID. Frozen scores are stationary, so the
pool is unbiased and the fit sharpens as $|\mathcal{S}_t|$ grows. The detached
Bayes posterior is the soft weight \eqref{eq:label},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:post}
\pi(\mathrm{OOD}\mid x_i)=
\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{ood}}\,\mathcal{N}(s_i\mid\mu_{\mathrm{ood}},\sigma_{\mathrm{ood}}^2)}
{\sum_c \pi_c\,\mathcal{N}(s_i\mid\mu_c,\sigma_c^2)},
\qquad \pi_{\mathrm{ID}}=1-\pi_{\mathrm{OOD}},
\end{equation}
never optimised---avoiding the hard-threshold errors the split-and-oppose
recipe propagates into its loss.

\paragraph{Loss: ID and OOD terms.} The loss is a soft-weighted sum of a
\csid{} term and a \csood{} term,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i
\Big[\,\pi_{\text{ID}}(x_i)\,H(p_i)
\;+\;\pi_{\text{OOD}}(x_i)\,\lambda\,\norm{\g(x_i)}_1\,\Big].
\end{equation}
The \csid{} term is \method{Tent}'s entropy minimisation weighted by
$\pi_{\text{ID}}$, so it acts mostly on likely-\csid{} and barely on
likely-\csood: it raises \csid{} accuracy while---unlike \method{Tent} on the
full batch---no longer sharpening \csood, and so stops degrading the OOD
signal further. The \csood{} term attacks a signal that is weak from the
start: covariate shift flattens the feature norm, so even the \emph{frozen}
energy AUROC is limited for lack of a norm gap. The $L_1$ penalty drives
likely-\csood{} norms down, manufacturing the gap \eqref{eq:gap} and
recovering OOD separation beyond what the frozen model alone gives.

\paragraph{Warm-up.} The score pool $\mathcal{S}_t$ grows as batches
accumulate, so early on the mixture \eqref{eq:gmm} is fit on few scores and
the posterior can sit far from the true membership. To stabilise adaptation
we trust the loss only gradually, ramping the learning rate over the first
$K$ steps,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:warmup}
\eta_t=\eta\cdot r(t/K),\qquad
r(u)=
\begin{cases}
u & \text{linear},\\[2pt]
\tfrac12\big(1-\cos\pi u\big) & \text{cosine},\\[2pt]
\dfrac{1-e^{-u/\tau}}{1-e^{-1/\tau}} & \text{exponential},
\end{cases}
\qquad r(u\!\ge\!1)=1 .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Results: \method{NOVA-TTA} vs.\ \method{Tent}}\label{sec:results}

explicit. \method{Tent} buys

separability. The $t{=}0$ baseline---BN statistics adapted, no gradient

the trend: penalising \csood{} norm not only halts the collapse but drives
AUROC \emph{above} the $t{=}0$ baseline to $0.93$, at negligible accuracy
cost.

\section{Conclusions and Discussion}

Closed-set TTA and open-set robustness pull in opposite geometric
directions. With a frozen head, entropy minimisation can only move the
feature norm or its angle; under covariate shift the norm gap is already
weak, so the damage is \emph{angular}---\csood{} inputs re-align onto the
nearest class weight and are absorbed into the source classes, until the
energy score can no longer separate them from \csid. We made this concrete
with a time-tracking protocol and a geometric analysis, then used it to
design \method{NOVA-TTA}, which supervises with a statistic the adaptation
cannot corrupt (a frozen, norm-invariant alignment score) and, unable to
undo the angular collapse, restores detection on the lever it \emph{can}
control---suppressing likely-\csood{} feature norm to re-open the gap.
\method{NOVA-TTA} reverses the collapse---AUROC $0.79\!\to\!0.93$ under
SVHN-C, $0.75\!\to\!0.84$ under Rome32---at no meaningful accuracy cost
(\S\ref{sec:nova-rome32}, App.~\ref{app:rome32}). The Rome32 sweep, on a
smaller and semantically closer \csood{} source under a shorter stream,
reproduces the trend on every one of the 15 corruptions. A natural next
step is a head-to-head against \method{UniEnt} under the same protocol.

\section{Rome32: Design and Adapted Protocol}\label{app:rome32}

\paragraph{Dataset design.} Rome32 is a custom \csood{} source assembled by
the research group from photographs collected around Rome and grouped into
five semantically disjoint scene categories---\texttt{affreschi} (frescoes),
\texttt{fontanelle} (drinking fountains), \texttt{monete} (coins),
\texttt{pasta}, and \texttt{statue}---plus a residual \texttt{\_ood} bucket of
images that did not fit any category. Each image was cropped and resized to
the $32\times32$ resolution of CIFAR-10 (\texttt{export32/} pipeline) and
then passed through the same corruption pipeline as CIFAR-10-C to yield a
covariate-shifted stream (Rome32-C). After excluding zero-byte placeholders
in the archive (the loader filters them at read time), the usable image
count per class is: \texttt{affreschi} 641, \texttt{fontanelle} 397,
\texttt{monete} 147, \texttt{pasta} 144, \texttt{statue} 525,
\texttt{\_ood} 743, for a total of \textbf{2{,}597} images. None of these
classes overlaps with the CIFAR-10 label set, so Rome32 is a valid open-set
\csood{} source.

\paragraph{Adapted protocol.} Rome32 holds two orders of magnitude fewer
samples than SVHN, so the open-set stream must be shorter and sparser in
\csood{} to keep adaptation \emph{zero-repeat} (every csOOD sample seen at
most once during a stream). Relative to the SVHN-C setting (\S\ref{sec:protocol},
App.~\ref{app:impl}) we keep the batch size $N{=}200$ but
(i) lower the per-batch \csood{} fraction to $\alpha=25/200=0.125$ (i.e.,
$25$ \csood{} + $175$ \csid{} per batch) and (ii) shorten the stream to
$T=20$ steps. This consumes $T\cdot n_{\text{ood}}=500$ csOOD draws out of
the $\sim597$-sample adapt pool ($2597$ total minus the $2000$ held out for
the diagnostic set), keeping the adaptation pass non-repeating. Everything
else---the two-phase time-tracking protocol, the frozen scorer, the
held-out diagnostic set ($2000$ csID + $2000$ csOOD, fixed indices), the
metrics---is unchanged, so the Rome32 trajectories are directly comparable
to the SVHN-C ones up to the $(T,\alpha)$ knobs.

\paragraph{Implementation.} The Rome32-specific stream defaults live in
\texttt{configs/stream\_rome32.yaml} and are read automatically by
\texttt{phase1\_adapt.py} when \texttt{csood\_source=rome32}; the
non-default $\alpha$ is plumbed through \texttt{build\_stream(\ldots,
n\_ood=25)}. Closed-set Rome32 runs are skipped (a closed-set stream never
touches the \csood{} pool, so it would duplicate the matching SVHN-C
closed-set run).
