\begin{abstract}
Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) keeps a deployed model accurate under
distribution shift. In the open world its success hinges on a second
requirement that is often ignored: the model must keep separating
in-distribution inputs from semantically novel out-of-distribution (OOD)
inputs. Existing open-set methods rely on high-confidence detection and
hard thresholds, and do not act on the feature norm directly, even though
the norm is the quantity adaptation moves most. We give a geometric
account of why the canonical entropy-based method (\method{Tent}) is
structurally at odds with OOD detection: with a frozen head, entropy
minimisation inflates feature norms uniformly across populations,
collapsing the norm gap that energy scores read. As our contribution we
introduce \method{Cassano}, a soft open-set TTA framework built around a
frozen, norm-invariant alignment score and a feature-norm objective:
likely-ID samples are adapted by entropy minimisation, likely-OOD samples
are regularised by an $L_1$ penalty on the feature norm that preserves the
OOD signal lost under covariate shift. On CIFAR-10-C, \method{Cassano}
reverses the collapse---energy-score AUROC rises over the stream where
\method{Tent} degrades---at no meaningful accuracy cost.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Deployed classifiers operate on streams that drift away from their
training distribution. Test-Time Adaptation responds by updating the model
online, without labels, as the test stream arrives. The canonical instance
is \method{Tent}~\cite{tent}: it minimises the entropy of the predictive
distribution one batch at a time, updating only the affine parameters of
the batch-normalisation (BN) layers. On closed-set corruption benchmarks
this is effective and cheap.

The closed-set assumption rarely survives deployment. A realistic stream is
\emph{open-set}: alongside corrupted but known-class inputs
(\emph{covariate-shifted in-distribution}, \csid) it contains semantically
novel inputs (\emph{covariate-shifted out-of-distribution}, \csood) that
the model should reject rather than classify. The prevailing remedy is a
\emph{split-and-oppose} recipe: estimate per-sample ID/OOD membership from
a score, minimise entropy on the presumed \csid{} subset, and oppose it on
the presumed \csood{} subset.

We argue that this framing hides a deeper, geometric failure that is
present even with a perfect split. With the head frozen, entropy
minimisation can only act on the feature norm and the angular alignment to
the class weights. The norm term dominates, and BN affine parameters scale
features uniformly across a population; entropy minimisation therefore
inflates \csood{} norms in lockstep with \csid{} norms, collapsing the
ID/OOD norm gap on which energy scores depend. Detectability degrades
regardless of split quality.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{Protocol.} A time-tracking, two-phase evaluation protocol
  that records a \emph{trajectory} of OOD and geometric metrics over the
  adaptation stream and separates persistent affine drift from transient
  BN statistics.
  \item \textbf{Analysis.} A geometric characterisation of \emph{why}
  closed-set TTA degrades open-set performance: norm inflation, norm-gap
  collapse, and angular relabelling.
  \item \textbf{Method.} \method{Cassano}, a soft open-set TTA framework
  whose every design choice follows from the failure analysis, and which
  empirically reverses the AUROC collapse.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Test-Time Adaptation.} \method{Tent}~\cite{tent} established
entropy minimisation over BN affine parameters as a strong, lightweight
baseline; \emph{BN Adapt}~\cite{bnadapt} is the gradient-free special case
that merely recomputes batch statistics. \method{UniEnt}~\cite{unient}
extends TTA to the open-set regime with an explicit ID/OOD split and
opposing per-subset objectives. We share their open-set goal but locate the
failure in the geometry of the affine update rather than in the split.

\paragraph{OOD detection.} Energy-based scoring~\cite{energy} reads a
log-partition energy off the logits (Eq.~\eqref{eq:energy}) and is a strong
post-hoc detector. It depends on an \csid/\csood{} gap in logit magnitude,
itself dominated by feature norm; we use the AUROC of the energy score
\eqref{eq:auroc} as the primary detectability metric.

\paragraph{Feature geometry.} The feature norm is a known ID/OOD
discriminator~\cite{featurenorm}: OOD inputs tend to produce
smaller-norm embeddings, and many post-hoc scores read this magnitude
indirectly. Relative representations~\cite{rrzs} encode a sample by its
cosine similarities to a fixed anchor set and are therefore invariant to
rescaling---the precise quantity a norm-dominant adaptation leaves
untouched. This motivates our use of a norm-invariant alignment statistic
as the scoring signal.

\section{TTA in the Closed and Open Set}\label{sec:tta}

\paragraph{Setting.} Let $f_\theta:\mathcal{X}\to\R^K$ be a $K$-class
classifier with logits $\psi_\theta(x)\in\R^K$ and predictive distribution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:softmax}
p_\theta(x)=\softmax\big(\psi_\theta(x)\big),
\qquad
p_{\theta,k}(x)=\frac{e^{\psi_k(x)}}{\sum_{j=1}^{K}e^{\psi_j(x)}} .
\end{equation}
TTA updates a small parameter subset $\theta$ online from an unlabelled
stream $\mathcal{B}_1,\dots,\mathcal{B}_T$ of batches of size $N$. Following
\method{Tent}, $\theta=(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})$ collects the affine
parameters of every batch-normalisation layer, which act channel-wise as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bn}
\mathrm{BN}(z)=\bm{\gamma}\odot
\frac{z-\bm{\mu}_{\mathcal{B}}}{\bm{\sigma}_{\mathcal{B}}}+\bm{\beta},
\end{equation}
the statistics $(\bm{\mu}_{\mathcal{B}},\bm{\sigma}_{\mathcal{B}})$ being
recomputed per batch (train mode) and never persisted, so only
$(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})$ accumulate across steps.

\paragraph{Closed vs.\ open set.} In the closed set every input is
covariate-shifted in-distribution (\csid) and the sole objective is
accuracy. In the open set the stream also carries covariate-shifted
out-of-distribution (\csood) inputs, from unknown classes, that must be
\emph{rejected}: a second objective appears, \emph{detectability}, the
separability of the two populations under a score $s:\mathcal{X}\to\R$,
which we quantify by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:auroc}
\AUROC(s)=\Pr\big[\,s(X_{\csid})>s(X_{\csood})\,\big],
\end{equation}
the Mann--Whitney probability that an \csid{} input outscores an \csood{}
one, estimated over $\D_{\csid}\times\D_{\csood}$.

\paragraph{Entropy minimisation (\method{Tent}).} \method{Tent}~\cite{tent}
takes one gradient step per batch on the mean predictive entropy,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tent}
\theta_t=\theta_{t-1}-\eta\,\nabla_{\theta}\,
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{B}_t}H\big(p_\theta(x)\big),
\qquad
H(p)=-\sum_{k=1}^{K}p_k\log p_k,
\end{equation}
with the head and convolutional weights frozen. Sharpening $p_\theta$ on
corrupted \csid{} inputs recovers most of the accuracy lost to covariate
shift. But \eqref{eq:tent} is blind to the \csid/\csood{} distinction:
entropy is minimised on \emph{every} sample, so the model is driven to be
confident on \csood{} inputs it should reject. \S\ref{sec:failure} shows
this is not benign mislabelling but a structural collapse of
\eqref{eq:auroc}: \method{Tent}'s energy-score AUROC falls monotonically
over the stream even as accuracy rises.

\section{Understanding the Failure of \method{Tent}}\label{sec:failure}

\subsection{Last-layer geometry}\label{sec:background}

Let $\g:\mathcal{X}\to\R^d$ be the penultimate embedding and
$\mathbf{W}=[\mathbf{w}_1,\dots,\mathbf{w}_K]^\top\in\R^{K\times d}$ the
frozen linear head with bias $\mathbf{b}$, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logits-def}
\psi(x)=\mathbf{W}\g(x)+\mathbf{b},
\qquad
\psi_k(x)=\mathbf{w}_k^\top\g(x)+b_k .
\end{equation}
Through \eqref{eq:softmax} the head is a multinomial logistic regression on
$\g(x)$: each $\mathbf{w}_k$ is the normal of the class-$k$ decision
hyperplane and the prediction is $c^*(x)=\argmax_k\psi_k(x)$. Writing the
winning logit in polar form around the embedding gives the decomposition
that organises the analysis,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logit}
\psi_{c^*}(x)=
\underbrace{\norm{\mathbf{w}_{c^*}}\,\norm{\g(x)}\,\cos\theta_{c^*}}
_{\text{norm}\,\times\,\text{alignment}}+\,b_{c^*},
\qquad
\cos\theta_{c^*}=\frac{\mathbf{w}_{c^*}^\top\g(x)}
{\norm{\mathbf{w}_{c^*}}\,\norm{\g(x)}} .
\end{equation}
With $\mathbf{W},\mathbf{b}$ frozen, the only levers entropy minimisation
can pull on $\psi_{c^*}$ are the feature norm $\norm{\g(x)}$ and the
alignment $\cos\theta_{c^*}$.

\subsection{OOD detection and the feature norm}\label{sec:ood}

Two scores read this geometry. \emph{Confidence} treats a peaked predictive
distribution as evidence of an ID input,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conf}
C(x)=\max_k p_{\theta,k}(x),
\end{equation}
the complement of predictive entropy. \emph{Energy}~\cite{energy} reads the
log-partition of the logits,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
\Energy(x)=-\log\sum_{k=1}^{K}e^{\psi_k(x)},
\end{equation}
a smooth maximum tracking the overall logit magnitude and hence, through
\eqref{eq:logit}, the feature norm $\norm{\g(x)}$. Since \csid{} inputs
carry larger embedding norm than \csood{} inputs~\cite{featurenorm}, the
detector $s(x)=-\Energy(x)$ separates the populations exactly to the extent
that the \emph{norm gap}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
\Delta(t)=\E_{\D_{\csid}}\norm{\gt(x)}-\E_{\D_{\csood}}\norm{\gt(x)}
\end{equation}
is positive. The open-set question is therefore whether adaptation
preserves $\Delta(t)$.

\subsection{Predicted effect of \method{Tent}}\label{sec:predict}

Entropy minimisation \eqref{eq:tent} lowers $H$ by sharpening $p_\theta$,
which by \eqref{eq:softmax}--\eqref{eq:logit} is achieved most cheaply by
raising the winning logit through its dominant factor $\norm{\g(x)}$. Yet
the affine parameters act as a channel-wise \emph{diagonal scaling}
\eqref{eq:bn}: to first order the adapted embedding is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaling}
\gt(x)\approx\mathbf{D}_t\,\gz(x),
\qquad
\mathbf{D}_t=\diag(\bm{d}_t),\ \ \bm{d}_t>\bm{0},
\end{equation}
a map applied to \emph{every} sample routed through the layer, \csood{}
included. Two consequences follow.

\emph{(a) Norm inflation is population-blind.} As the entries of
$\bm{d}_t$ grow, $\norm{\gt(x)}\!\approx\!\norm{\mathbf{D}_t\gz(x)}$ rises
for \csid{} and \csood{} alike, so the gap \eqref{eq:gap} contracts toward
zero and the energy detector $-\Energy$ degrades.

\emph{(b) Angle is invariant to uniform scaling.} For an isotropic
$\mathbf{D}_t=c\,\mathbf{I}$ the cosine alignment in \eqref{eq:logit} is
unchanged,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cos-invariant}
\frac{\mathbf{w}_k^\top\!\big(c\,\g(x)\big)}
{\norm{\mathbf{w}_k}\,\norm{c\,\g(x)}}
=\frac{\mathbf{w}_k^\top\g(x)}{\norm{\mathbf{w}_k}\,\norm{\g(x)}},
\qquad c>0 .
\end{equation}
The two objectives are thus geometrically opposed: accuracy is bought by
the very norm inflation that closes the energy gap \eqref{eq:gap}, so we
predict $\AUROC(t)\!\downarrow$ while $\mathrm{Acc}(t)\!\uparrow$
(confirmed in \S\ref{sec:results}, Fig.~\ref{fig:exp1}). Equally,
\eqref{eq:cos-invariant} shows a cosine statistic is immune to exactly the
lever that breaks energy---the seed of the fix in \S\ref{sec:cassano}.

\section{Experiment on the Failure of \method{Tent}}\label{sec:experiment}

\subsection{Time-tracking protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

We separate adaptation from evaluation so that any change in a metric
reflects only the persistent affine drift.

\paragraph{Phase 1 (adaptation).} Run a method on a stream of $T$ batches
and save the BN affine state $\theta_t$ after each step, yielding $T{+}1$
checkpoints $\theta_0,\dots,\theta_T$.

\paragraph{Phase 2 (analysis).} Load each checkpoint, freeze it, and
evaluate it on a fixed held-out diagnostic set
$\D=\D_{\csid}\cup\D_{\csood}$ (2000$+$2000 examples) that is never adapted
on. Any change in a trajectory $m(0),\dots,m(T)$ then reflects only the
drift $\theta_{t-1}\!\to\!\theta_t$. The diagnostic set is evaluated in
$20$ fixed mixed batches of $100\,\csid+100\,\csood$---the same size and
ID/OOD regime as adaptation---so BN statistics are computed under
deployment-realistic conditions. A single large batch would change the
effective feature space; evaluating the populations separately would let
each be normalised by its own statistics and artificially stabilise the
\csood{} features, masking the norm-gap collapse we measure.

\paragraph{Setup.} A stream is fixed by
$(\text{method},\text{corruption},\text{\csood source},\text{open\_set},
\text{seed})$. Unless noted we use CIFAR-10-C \texttt{gaussian\_noise} at
severity~$5$ as \csid{} and SVHN-C as \csood, balanced open-set, $N{=}200$,
$T{=}80$ with no repeated examples, seed~$0$. The backbone is
WideResNet-40-2 pretrained with AugMix+JSD~\cite{augmix} on CIFAR-10
(RobustBench~\cite{robustbench}, clean accuracy $\approx 95.8\%$).

\subsection{Metrics}\label{sec:metrics}

Every metric is a function of a frozen checkpoint $\theta_t$ and the fixed
diagnostic set; $\E_{\D_\bullet}$ denotes a mean over a population
$\bullet\in\{\csid,\csood\}$, and $c^*_0(x)=\argmax_k\psi_0(x)$ is the
\emph{source}-predicted class, frozen at $t{=}0$.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep4pt
\item \textbf{Detectability and accuracy.} $\AUROC(t)$ from
\eqref{eq:auroc} with $s=-\Energy$, and
$\mathrm{Acc}(t)=\E_{\D_{\csid}}\Ind\!\big[c^*_t(x)=y\big]$.

\item \textbf{Feature norms and gap.} Mean $L_2$ and $L_1$ norms per
population, $\E_{\D_\bullet}\norm{\gt(x)}_2$ and
$\E_{\D_\bullet}\norm{\gt(x)}_1$, with gaps $\Delta_2(t),\Delta_1(t)$ as in
\eqref{eq:gap}.

\item \textbf{Alignment.} Cosine to the \emph{source}-predicted class,
$\E_{\D_\bullet}\!\big[\cos\theta_{c^*_0(x)}\big]$, and the norm-invariant
max cosine $\E_{\D_\bullet}\!\big[\max_k \cos\angle(\gt(x),\mathbf{w}_k)\big]$
(the score of Eq.~\eqref{eq:score}).

\item \textbf{Semantics and confidence.} Mean distance to the nearest
frozen source centroid,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centroid}
\E_{\D_\bullet}\min_{c}\norm{\gt(x)-\bm{\mu}_c^{(0)}},
\qquad
\bm{\mu}_c^{(0)}=\E\big[\gz(x)\mid y=c\big]\ \text{(clean CIFAR-10)};
\end{equation}
the \csood{} confidence $\E_{\D_{\csood}}C(x)$ from \eqref{eq:conf}; and the
predicted-class churn $\E_{\D_{\csood}}\Ind\!\big[c^*_t(x)\neq c^*_{t-1}(x)\big]$.
\end{itemize}

\noindent At $t{=}0$ we also report the fraction of each population on the
positive side of its predicted hyperplane,
$F_\bullet=\E_{\D_\bullet}\Ind\!\big[\cos\theta_{c^*_0(x)}>0\big]$, which
conditions the reading of the angular metrics.

The diagnostics confirm the prediction of \S\ref{sec:predict} and add
detail. \emph{(i) Norm inflation is uniform and the gap collapses}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:geo1}): both populations' norms rise, the \csid$-$\csood{}
gap shrinks toward zero around $t\!\approx\!25$, and the $L_1$ gap goes
\emph{negative}---\csood{} features become larger in $L_1$ than \csid{}
ones. The feature norm is therefore not a reliable signal in our setting:
adaptation moves it and can invert it. \emph{(ii) Adaptation is angular
relabelling, not absorption} (Fig.~\ref{fig:geo2}): the cosine to the
source-predicted class drops for \csood{} while the max cosine over all
weights rises; \csood{} samples are re-aligned to whichever weight is
nearest, with confidence climbing, while centroid distances stay flat.

Two facts survive the analysis and motivate the method. First, at $t{=}0$
the \emph{frozen} max-cosine statistic already separates the populations
(Fig.~\ref{fig:maxcos}). Second, this statistic is norm-invariant and,
computed by a frozen model, stationary across $t$---immune to the very norm
inflation that destroys energy scoring.

\section{Our Proposal: \method{Cassano}}\label{sec:cassano}

\subsection{A general framework}\label{sec:framework}

We cast open-set TTA as a pipeline
\[
s(x)\;\longrightarrow\;\pi(\mathrm{OOD}\mid x)\;\longrightarrow\;
w_i\;\longrightarrow\;\mathcal{L}.
\]
A score $s(x)$ yields a posterior $\pi(\mathrm{OOD}\mid x)$, which is turned
into a per-sample membership weight---hard or soft---
\begin{equation}\label{eq:label}
w_i=
\begin{cases}
\Ind\!\big[\pi(\mathrm{OOD}\mid x_i)>\tau\big] & \text{(hard split)},\\[3pt]
\pi(\mathrm{OOD}\mid x_i)\in[0,1] & \text{(soft weight)},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and $w_i$ gates an objective that adapts on \csid{} and opposes on \csood.
\method{UniEnt} instantiates this with an energy score and a hard split;
\method{Cassano} with a frozen alignment score and a soft weight, each
choice dictated by \S\ref{sec:failure}.

\subsection{Method}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Score: frozen, norm-invariant alignment.} A \emph{frozen} scorer
(original weights $\theta_0$, BN batch statistics, no gradient) runs
alongside the adapted model and produces the max cosine to the class
weights,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score}
s_i=\max_k \frac{\g(x_i)\cdot\mathbf{w}_k}
{\norm{\g(x_i)}\,\norm{\mathbf{w}_k}} .
\end{equation}
This is the one statistic the analysis showed to be both discriminative at
$t{=}0$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:maxcos}) and immune to adaptation: it is
norm-invariant, so it ignores the inflation of finding~(i), and it is
computed by a frozen model, so scores pooled across steps are unbiased---unlike
the energy score, which is corrupted by the very adaptation it would
supervise.

\paragraph{Posterior: soft labelling.} Pool the scores seen up to step $t$,
$\mathcal{S}_t=\bigcup_{\tau\le t}\{s_i\}_{i\in\mathcal{B}_\tau}$, and fit by
EM a two-component Gaussian mixture
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gmm}
p(s)=\sum_{c\in\{\mathrm{id},\mathrm{ood}\}}
\pi_c\,\mathcal{N}\!\big(s\mid\mu_c,\sigma_c^2\big),
\qquad
\mu_{\mathrm{id}}>\mu_{\mathrm{ood}} ,
\end{equation}
the higher-mean component being ID. Because the scorer is frozen, the
score law is stationary across $t$, so pooling in \eqref{eq:gmm} is
unbiased and the fit sharpens as $|\mathcal{S}_t|$ grows. The detached Bayes
posterior gives the soft weight \eqref{eq:label},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:post}
\pi(\mathrm{OOD}\mid x_i)=
\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{ood}}\,\mathcal{N}(s_i\mid\mu_{\mathrm{ood}},\sigma_{\mathrm{ood}}^2)}
{\sum_c \pi_c\,\mathcal{N}(s_i\mid\mu_c,\sigma_c^2)},
\qquad \pi_{\mathrm{ID}}=1-\pi_{\mathrm{OOD}},
\end{equation}
used as a weight, never optimised---avoiding the hard-threshold errors that
the split-and-oppose recipe propagates into its objective.

\paragraph{Objective: oppose on the failure lever.} The two findings
dictate the two terms:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i
\Big[\,\pi_{\text{ID}}(x_i)\,H(p_i)
\;+\;\pi_{\text{OOD}}(x_i)\,\lambda\,\norm{\g(x_i)}_1\,\Big].
\end{equation}
On likely-\csid{} samples we minimise entropy---the useful part of
\method{Tent}. On likely-\csood{} samples we penalise the $L_1$ feature
norm, acting directly on the lever \eqref{eq:scaling} identified: rather
than hoping a hard split prevents norm inflation, we suppress \csood{} norms
and re-open the gap \eqref{eq:gap}. Since $H\le\ln 10\approx2.30$ while
$\norm{\g}_1\approx 28$--$30$, we set $\lambda\approx 1/30$ to balance the
two terms. While the pool $\mathcal{S}_t$---and hence the mixture
\eqref{eq:gmm}---is still small, the posterior is noisy; we damp it with a
learning-rate warm-up over the first $K$ steps,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:warmup}
\eta_t=\eta\cdot r(t/K),\qquad
r(u)=
\begin{cases}
u & \text{linear},\\[2pt]
\tfrac12\big(1-\cos\pi u\big) & \text{cosine},\\[2pt]
\dfrac{1-e^{-u/\tau}}{1-e^{-1/\tau}} & \text{exponential},
\end{cases}
\qquad r(u\!\ge\!1)=1 .
\end{equation}

    \method{Tent}     & $0.79\to\mathbf{0.69}$ & $0.77\to0.83$\\
    \method{Cassano}  & $0.78\to\mathbf{0.94}$ & $0.77\to0.80$\\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{AUROC vs.\ accuracy.} Figure~\ref{fig:exp1} and
Table~\ref{tab:endpoints} make the trade-off explicit. \method{Tent} buys
$+6$pt accuracy with a $-10$pt AUROC loss: the objectives are orthogonal,
and entropy minimisation optimises accuracy at the direct expense of
separability. \emph{BN Adapt} isolates the cause---with no gradient step
its AUROC is perfectly flat, so the degradation is entirely due to the
persistent $(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})$ affine drift. \method{Cassano} inverts
the trend: penalising \csood{} norm not only halts the collapse but drives
AUROC \emph{above} the $t{=}0$ baseline to $0.94$, at negligible accuracy
cost.

\paragraph{Geometric reading.} The diagnostics of \S\ref{sec:failure}
explain the gap. \method{Tent} inflates \csood{} norms and relabels them
angularly; \method{Cassano}'s $L_1$ penalty keeps likely-\csood{} norms
suppressed, restoring the energy gap the detector reads. Against the hard
split-and-oppose family, \method{Cassano}'s advantage is twofold: its score
is frozen and norm-invariant rather than corrupted by the adaptation it
supervises, and its labelling is a soft posterior rather than a hard
threshold, so \eqref{eq:loss} degrades gracefully under split uncertainty
instead of compounding split errors.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusions and Discussion}

Closed-set TTA and open-set robustness pull in opposite geometric
directions: with a frozen head, entropy minimisation over BN affine
parameters inflates feature norms uniformly, collapsing the energy gap that
OOD detection relies on. The dangerous degree of freedom is the
\emph{global} feature scale, and detectors that read it (energy, max-logit)
are self-defeating under adaptation. We made this concrete with a
time-tracking protocol and a geometric analysis, then used the analysis to
design \method{Cassano}, which supervises with a statistic the adaptation
cannot corrupt (a frozen, norm-invariant alignment score) and acts on the
exact lever the failure uses (the $L_1$ norm). \method{Cassano} reverses
the collapse---AUROC $0.79\!\to\!0.94$---at no meaningful accuracy cost.
Natural next steps are the full corruption sweep, a second \csood{} source
(Rome32), and a head-to-head against \method{UniEnt} under this protocol.
